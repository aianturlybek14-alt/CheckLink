Scam schemes and how to spot them

CheckLink guide: the most common scams in Kazakhstan, their signs and what to do. You can check a suspicious text for free, without signing up.

Check a message or link for free

  • Phishing SMS and links — Scammers send SMS and messages in the name of banks, eGov and couriers with a link to a fake site. The goal is to get your card details, login or confirmation code.
  • A call “from the bank” or “security department” — The caller claims to be from a bank, the police or the KNB, scares you with a “suspicious transaction” and asks for a code, card details, a transfer or an app install.
  • The “safe account” — You’re told your money is “being stolen” and asked to move it to a “safe”, “reserve” or “protected” account. Such accounts don’t exist: the money goes to scammers.
  • “A relative in trouble” — Someone writes or calls as a relative from a “new number”: an accident, the police, a broken phone — and money is needed urgently. Sometimes a fake voice is used.
  • SMS and push codes are for you only — An SMS or push code confirms a login or a payment. Whoever gets it can enter your account or make a transaction in your name.
  • How to spot a fake website — A fake looks like the original, but the address is different. The key is the domain: the part before the first single slash. For Kaspi it’s kaspi.kz, for eGov it’s egov.kz.
  • QR codes — A QR code is just a link or text. Scammers stick their own codes over real ones on parking meters, in cafés and on ads to lead you to a fake payment page.
  • APK and dangerous files — An .apk outside the app store, a program disguised as a document (.pdf.exe) or a document with macros can infect your device: read SMS and codes and control your banking app.
  • Remote access apps — AnyDesk, RustDesk, TeamViewer and similar apps give another person full control of your phone. Scammers ask you to install them “to protect your account” or “for a check”.
  • Hijacked WhatsApp and Telegram — Accounts are hijacked by tricking you into sharing the login code: “forward the code, it came by mistake”, “vote for my niece”, “confirm the login”. Then friends are asked for money in your name.
  • Investments and “guaranteed returns” — You’re offered to invest in a “famous company project”, crypto or “exchange trading” with a guaranteed high return. The first payouts are made so you invest more.
  • “Jobs” that require you to pay — You’re offered easy side work: likes, reviews, “buying out goods”. At first they pay a little, then ask you to deposit money for “access”, “insurance” or a “level up”.
  • Buying and selling via classifieds — On OLX, Kolesa and Krisha scammers pose as buyers and send a “link to receive money”, or ask for prepayment for goods that don’t exist.
  • Fake online shops — Fake shops on Instagram and new websites sell gadgets and clothes “70% off”. After you pay by card transfer, the seller disappears.
  • Prizes, bonuses and compensation — You’re told about a prize, “compensation” or “cashback” and asked to pay for delivery or tax, or to enter card details to receive it.
  • Messages “from eGov” and government bodies — Scammers write about a “tax refund”, a “fine”, a “benefit” or a “blocked digital signature” with a link to a site that looks like egov.kz.
  • A loan in your name — They call to say a loan is “being taken out” in your name and offer to “cancel” it with a counter-loan and a transfer to a “protected account”.
  • Strong passwords — Short and personal passwords (a name, a birth date, qwerty) are guessed in seconds. One password everywhere means one leak opens everything.
  • Two-factor protection — Two-factor protection asks for a code or confirmation on your phone in addition to the password. Even a stolen password won’t help an attacker.
  • If you’ve already been scammed — Act immediately: the sooner your bank knows about a fraudulent transfer, the better the chance to stop it.
  • Public Wi-Fi — In open networks at cafés, airports and malls an attacker can set up a twin hotspot to snoop on data or show a fake login page.
  • Protecting older relatives and children — Scammers most often call older people and message children in games and social media. The main protection is to explain the schemes in advance and agree on rules.
